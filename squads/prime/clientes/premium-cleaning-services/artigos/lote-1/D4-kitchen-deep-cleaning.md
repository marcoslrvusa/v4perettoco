## 1. What is the commercial kitchen deep cleaning protocol?

A commercial kitchen deep cleaning protocol is the documented scope of work that separates deep cleaning from routine nightly sanitation in a professional kitchen.

The protocol answers five questions in writing: what gets cleaned, when it gets cleaned, with what it gets cleaned, how the work is sequenced, and how the completion is recorded.

The first answer is scope. A deep cleaning protocol names the zones it covers, including the cooking line, the fryer and grill lines, the wok line, the prep surfaces, the shelving exteriors, the tile and grout, and the floor systems that carry cooking soil.

The second answer is timing. The protocol assigns the deep clean to a cadence, usually weekly or monthly, and connects that cadence to the cooking volume of the operation.

The third answer is method. The protocol selects degreasing, extraction, stainless detailing and floor sanitation, and assigns chemistry and equipment to each task.

The fourth answer is sequencing. The deep clean runs after close when the kitchen must reopen the same morning, or on a scheduled closure window when the operation allows it.

The fifth answer is evidence. The protocol ends with checklists signed per visit, dated logs and manager review, which is the documentation that health inspections and insurance reviews expect from a cleaning program.

The distinction between cleaning and hygiene is operational rather than editorial, and it has direct consequences for the deep cleaning scope.

Cleaning removes soil that has accumulated. Sanitation and degreasing manage the conditions that keep soil from returning, and food handling stays under the certified food-service scope.

The deep cleaning protocol must state which surfaces it covers and which surfaces it leaves to the food-service operation, because the cleaning provider and the kitchen's certified food handlers are accountable for different surfaces.

The protocol is also the unit on which pricing, scheduling and auditing are built.

A deep clean quoted without a protocol is a price. A deep clean defined by a protocol is a service, and the difference is what lets a facility manager compare proposals, defend an invoice and train a crew.

Deep cleaning in the commercial kitchen is not a second nightly run with stronger products.

It is a different category of work, and treating it as a bigger nightly clean is the most common error in restaurant cleaning programs.

The protocol gives the cleaning crew a single source of truth for the deep clean, which is what allows a vendor to train a crew, price a job and audit a result without re-deriving the scope each time.

The protocol should also carry the kitchen's cooking schedule into the deep clean planning, because the deep clean has to be sequenced around the service that produced the soil.

## 2. How does the nightly kitchen deep cleaning protocol run after close?

The nightly kitchen deep cleaning protocol runs after close and before the next service period.

The sequence starts with trash, with trash pull and liner reset and trash staging away from food preparation areas.

The dining floor takes vacuum and mop work, and chair and baseboard touch-ups follow.

Restrooms are disinfected and restocked, with mop separation by zone, meaning restroom cleaning chemistry stays away from food surfaces.

Entrance glass and handles receive cleaning, and an odor check closes the daily pass.

A photo log of completed zones supports the daily record, and the daily record supports the deeper cleaning program.

Fryer areas, hood systems and food-contact prep surfaces remain with the food-service team unless a separate certified scope is signed.

The cleaning provider does not take ownership of food handling, and the deep cleaning protocol does not cross that line.

The nightly run is sequenced so that the deep cleaning work does not compete with the daily sanitation work during service.

Overnight restaurant cleaning in New Jersey is built on this post-close rhythm, with night crews covering dining room resets, bar area detailing, restroom sanitizing, floor care, trash removal and, for hospitality accounts, guest room turnovers after closing.
